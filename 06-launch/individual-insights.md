# Individual Insights (Module 6)

## Friction points

- The original projects optimised different strengths: one had better persona–solution fit; the other had a stronger strategic business case and more observable metrics.
- The initial payment-share metric required data Riverty cannot directly observe across Klarna, PayPal, and bank cards.
- Platform choice was initially treated as a product fact before cohort device mix was validated.
- A usability prototype can test comprehension and recovery states, but not live tokenisation, payment behaviour, retention, or merchant cannibalisation.

## Key learnings

- Separate the **design persona** from the **experiment cohort**.
- A narrow feature needs a metric that the product can actually observe.
- Mechanism metrics explain why an experiment moved; they should not replace the business outcome.
- One decision-relevant guardrail is stronger than several unpowered metrics.
- Synthetic evidence is useful for planning only when it is clearly labelled and validated before launch.

## Aha moment

Immediate wallet access is not valuable simply because it is convenient. It creates value only if earlier availability builds a lasting payment habit without moving profitable volume away from partner checkout.

## What we would do next

1. Confirm the pilot cohort, approval funnel, device mix, and first supported wallet.
2. Validate the baseline, MDE, achievable sample, guardrail variance, and operational dependencies.
3. Run the usability gate and friends-and-family beta.
4. Pre-register the experiment definitions and decision rules.
5. Scale only if sustained usage improves and the merchant guardrail holds.
