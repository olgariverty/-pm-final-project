Slide 5 · Strategy

Riverty disappears when partner checkout does

Customer problem

Lena uses whichever option is available. Where merchants do not offer Riverty, she falls back to Klarna, PayPal, or a bank card.

Strategic opportunity

One supported phone wallet can create an in-store route for an issued Riverty Card—without changing the underlying credit proposition.

Causal bet

Wallet provisioning will increase repeat card use beyond partner checkout while the merchant channel remains protected.

Strategic test: Can Riverty build a direct, repeat consumer relationship without weakening its merchant-first channel?

Slide 6 · Research

Lena's relevant friction is acceptance beyond partner checkout

Persona Case

Lena, 32, Cologne. Frequent BNPL user who shops mainly on her phone.

Workaround Case

Klarna, PayPal, or an existing debit or credit card.

Boundary

Wallet provisioning expands acceptance. It does not itself solve pay-after-deciding.

Slide 8 · Validation

Will wallet access create durable usage without cannibalising partner checkout?

Control A

Same eligible, activated cardholder and current card experience—without phone-wallet provisioning during the test.

Variant B

Same cardholder and card experience—plus one supported phone-wallet provisioning flow.

Primary

M3 card retention: randomised activated cardholders with ≥1 card purchase in the third 30-day period.

Guardrail

Partner-checkout spend per randomised participant: proposed boundary ≤3% below control.
