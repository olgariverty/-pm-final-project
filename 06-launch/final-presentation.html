Slide 5 · Strategy

Riverty can grow beyond partner checkout—but only if the card creates incremental usage.

Customer problem

Lena takes whichever BNPL provider appears at checkout. Riverty reaches her only through partner merchants.

Strategic opportunity

A consumer card could give frequent users a reason to choose Riverty in more everyday moments.

Key hypothesis

Prioritising loyal frequent users for the card will build repeat usage beyond partner checkout, without weakening partner checkout.

Slide 6 · Research

Lena’s behaviour shows an acceptance gap, not proven brand preference.

Persona and goal

Lena, 32, Cologne. Pays only after deciding to keep an item.

Current workaround

Klarna, PayPal or an existing debit/credit card.

Core friction

Riverty is available mainly through partner checkout.

  2. Does it divert partner checkout?
  3. Does she choose Riverty unprompted?

Slide 10 · Story

The card creates value only if broader acceptance adds usage without cannibalisation.

Aha moment

The card itself is not the value proposition—broader everyday acceptance is. It creates value only if it adds usage without cannibalising partner checkout.

Three lessons
  1. Lena chooses by availability, not preference.
  2. The evidence is synthetic; test before scaling.
  3. The prototype validates flow usability only.
Three next steps
  1. Validate provisioning in the friends-and-family beta.
  2. Run the experiment against the metric and guardrail.
  3. Confirm Compliance and wallet dependencies.
