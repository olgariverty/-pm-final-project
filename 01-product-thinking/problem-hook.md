# Problem Hook and Value Proposition

## Persona

Lena, 32, Cologne, is a frequent BNPL user who shops mainly on her phone. Her goal is to pay only after deciding to keep an item.

## Problem hook

Lena uses BNPL on most non-trivial purchases but takes whichever provider appears at checkout. She knows Riverty, but rarely seeks it out, so Riverty only reaches her where partner merchants show it. A card could give her a reason to choose Riverty beyond partner checkout.

## Strategic crisis

If Riverty does nothing for 12 months, it remains dependent on merchant checkout to reach consumers, with limited presence in everyday and in-store payments. It may miss the opportunity to build a direct relationship with frequent BNPL users, while alternatives such as Klarna and PayPal remain available. The scale of this risk still needs validation.

## Moment of misery

Where a partner merchant does not offer Riverty, Lena falls back to Klarna, PayPal, or a bank card.

Frequent Riverty users may also want to pay with Riverty outside partner checkout but have no way to do so (hypothesis to validate).

## Value proposition

For Lena, a Riverty card could give her a reason to use Riverty beyond partner checkout.

**Why now:** Riverty remains dependent on merchant checkout while Klarna and PayPal remain available.

This is a proposition to validate, not a confirmed user need.

## Scope boundary

Phone-wallet provisioning addresses acceptance beyond partner checkout, especially in store. It does **not** solve Lena's separate goal of paying only after deciding what to keep; that need is unvalidated and requires separate validation against the card's repayment terms.

## Strategic question

Can phone-wallet access create durable card usage beyond partner checkout without reducing existing partner-checkout spend?
