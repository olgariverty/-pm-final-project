# Strategic Discovery Map (Module 1)

## Responses
- **Write one feature from the map that, if an engineer asked “why is this a priority for our 12-week roadmap?”, you honestly could not give a strategic answer for.:** Rewards. I put it on the map because fitness and habit apps usually have it, not because I had a reason tied to this product. If an engineer asked why it's worth 12 weeks of roadmap, I would have nothing real to say.
- **Write one feature that looks “correct” for the app but has zero strategic weight, you have no idea if it helps the company make money or whether a single user actually asked for it.:** Seasonal bottle skins. Users can customize the on-screen water bottle with animated seasonal themes.
This reads like a real roadmap item and it has no strategic weight because of no user asked for it. It exists just because competitors have something similar and it sounded fun.
- **Compare V3 to your Step 1 baseline. What changed, and is the output objectively better, did the AI get smarter, or did your added PM knowledge (JTBD, effort/impact, retention) force it to be useful?:** V3 is better for its goal, a discovery sprint, but the model didn't get smarter. Prompt did the work.
- **Key takeaway: how does this prove the PM’s role is to think while the AI’s role is to execute?:** AI can organize ideas, but PM provides the case context, sets the decision criteria and judges which opportunities are worth validating.
