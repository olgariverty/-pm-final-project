# Lena's Journey: Activation to Repeat Use

**Design persona:** Lena, 32, Cologne, a frequent mobile BNPL user.
**Goal:** Pay only after deciding to keep an item.
**Value proposition:** For Lena, a Riverty card could give her a reason to use Riverty beyond partner checkout.
**Experiment cohort:** Eligible, activated, loyal frequent Riverty cardholders with a compatible device. Lena informs the design; she is not the targeting rule.

**Evidence tags:** the *Current friction* column is case input. The *Proposed experience* and *What we learn* columns are hypotheses to validate.

| Stage | User action | Current friction | Proposed experience | What we learn |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Activate** | Activates the issued Riverty Card | Riverty remains tied to partner checkout or existing card channels | Eligible users enter the controlled pilot | Whether the cohort is reachable and correctly instrumented |
| **2. Provision** | Adds the card to one supported phone wallet | No wallet route for using Riverty in store | Clear setup, success state, or actionable recovery | Whether provisioning is understandable and reliable |
| **3. Use in store** (moment of misery) | Pays at a compatible terminal | Falls back to another provider or bank card where Riverty is unavailable | Uses the issued Riverty Card through the wallet | Whether the feature creates incremental in-store usage |
| **4. Repeat** | Makes a card purchase during days 61–90 | Riverty may remain occasional rather than habitual | Repeats card use beyond partner checkout | Whether usage persists, not merely activates once |

## Moment of misery

Where a partner merchant does not offer Riverty, Lena falls back to Klarna, PayPal, or a bank card.

## Evidence boundary

The journey tests acceptance and repeat use. It does not promise pay-after-deciding, new credit terms, universal acceptance, or loyalty rewards.
